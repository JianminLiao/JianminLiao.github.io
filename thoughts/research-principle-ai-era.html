A Research Principle for the AI Era

Jianmin Liao · September 28, 2026

Read PDFLaTeX source

Research in the AI era should pursue intrinsic value while building the external support needed to sustain that pursuit.

Even when intrinsic value is the sole objective, an initial phase devoted entirely to external return can be necessary for optimal research. The model below makes this statement precise: with a sufficiently long horizon, every optimal strategy begins with full external focus, followed by an optimal phase that combines intrinsic work with the external return required to maintain a growing safety balance.

Here, intrinsic value means the scientific understanding or other substantive contribution that the researcher ultimately values. External return means support convertible into research funding, measured in the same units as expenditure. The distinction separates the purpose of research from the resources that sustain it.

Assumptions

Consider a fixed number nn of research cycles. In cycle ii, let cic_i be expenditure and pi∈[0,1]p_i\in[0,1] the share allocated to intrinsic work. Write IiI_i and EiE_i for intrinsic value and external return, respectively.

1. Linear production and funding renewal. Choose units so that c1=1c_1=1 and

Ii=pici,I_i=p_i c_i,
Ei=b(1−pi)ci,E_i=b(1-p_i)c_i,
ci+1=Ei.c_{i+1}=E_i.

The first two relations give a constant return per unit allocated to either activity. The third makes the next cycle's research budget equal to the current external return. For the final cycle, cn+1c_{n+1} is only a convenient name for EnE_n.

2. A positive and growing safety balance. Let BiB_i denote the cumulative funding surplus at the end of cycle ii:

B0=0,B_0=0,
Bi=Bi−1+Ei−ci.B_i=B_{i-1}+E_i-c_i.

Require

Bi≥max⁡{δ,ρBi−1},B_i\ge\max\{\delta,\rho B_{i-1}\},
0<δ≤b−1,0<\delta\le b-1,
1<ρ<b.1<\rho<b.

The fixed floor δ\delta establishes a safety reserve, while ρ\rho requires it to grow. The upper bound on δ\delta makes the first cycle feasible. The inequality ρ<b\rho<b leaves room to produce intrinsic value while meeting the growth requirement. Funding is assessed at cycle boundaries; the initial expenditure is supplied as seed funding.

3. Intrinsic value as the sole objective. Choose the allocation shares to maximize

∑i=1nIi.\sum_{i=1}^{n} I_i.

Returns are deterministic, each cycle's intrinsic value receives equal weight, and external return has value through the funding dynamics and constraints.

4. Enough time for reinvestment. Define

K=min⁡{m≥1:(b−ρ)∑j=0m−1ρj>1},K=\min\left\{m\ge1:(b-\rho)\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\rho^j>1\right\},
n>K.n>K.

Here mm is an integer. Since b>ρ>1b>\rho>1, KK is finite. This assumption gives early external returns enough time to generate later intrinsic value.

Result

Theorem

Under these assumptions, every optimal strategy devotes the first n−Kn-K cycles entirely to external return:

pi=0p_i=0
(1≤i≤n−K).(1\le i\le n-K).

There is an optimal strategy that then meets the balance-growth constraint exactly in all remaining cycles:

Bi=ρBi−1,B_i=\rho B_{i-1},
pi=1−1+ρBi−1b(1+Bi−1)∈(0,1)p_i=1-\frac{1+\rho B_{i-1}}{b(1+B_{i-1})}\in(0,1)
(n−K<i≤n).(n-K<i\le n).

Thus, maximizing intrinsic value alone requires an initial phase of full external focus and admits a single subsequent switch to a mixture of intrinsic and external work.

Proof

The funding identity telescopes to

Bi=ci+1−1,B_i=c_{i+1}-1,
ci=1+Bi−1.c_i=1+B_{i-1}.

Consequently, at any cycle after the first, a current balance B≥δB\ge\delta permits exactly the next balances B′B' satisfying

ρB≤B′≤bB+b−1.\rho B\le B'\le bB+b-1.

The intrinsic value earned in that cycle is

I=1−1b+B−B′b.I=1-\frac1b+B-\frac{B'}b.

The upper endpoint corresponds to p=0p=0; the lower endpoint meets the required balance growth exactly.

Let Vm(B)V_m(B) be the largest total intrinsic value attainable with mm cycles remaining and current balance B≥δB\ge\delta. We show by induction that VmV_m is affine in BB, and denote its slope by AmA_m. Since V0=0V_0=0, A0=0A_0=0. The recursion is

Vm(B)=1−1b+B+max⁡ρB≤B′≤bB+b−1{Vm−1(B′)−B′b}.V_m(B)=1-\frac1b+B+ \max_{\rho B\le B'\le bB+b-1} \left\{V_{m-1}(B')-\frac{B'}b\right\}.

If Vm−1V_{m-1} is affine, the coefficient of B′B' is Am−1−1/bA_{m-1}-1/b. Therefore the maximizing choice is the upper endpoint when Am−1>1/bA_{m-1}>1/b, and the lower endpoint when Am−1<1/bA_{m-1}<1/b. At equality, either endpoint is optimal; choose the lower one. Substitution shows that VmV_m is affine and gives

Am={1−ρ/b+ρAm−1,Am−1≤1/b,bAm−1,Am−1>1/b.A_m= \begin{cases} 1-\rho/b+\rho A_{m-1},&A_{m-1}\le1/b,\\ bA_{m-1},&A_{m-1}>1/b. \end{cases}

Until the threshold is crossed,

Am=b−ρb∑j=0m−1ρj.A_m=\frac{b-\rho}{b}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\rho^j.

Hence AK−1≤1/b<AKA_{K-1}\le1/b<A_K. After this crossing, the second branch keeps Am>1/bA_m>1/b. It follows that every cycle with more than KK cycles remaining has the unique optimal choice of full external focus. With at most KK cycles remaining, choosing the lower endpoint is optimal.

For the first cycle, the feasible interval is δ≤B1≤b−1\delta\le B_1\le b-1. Its objective has coefficient An−1−1/bA_{n-1}-1/b on B1B_1. Since n>Kn>K, this coefficient is strictly positive, so every optimum chooses B1=b−1B_1=b-1, equivalently p1=0p_1=0. The same argument applies to the subsequent cycles with more than KK cycles remaining.

In the last KK cycles, choose Bi=ρBi−1B_i=\rho B_{i-1}. The formula for pip_i follows from Ei=1+Bi=b(1−pi)(1+Bi−1)E_i=1+B_i=b(1-p_i)(1+B_{i-1}). Since Bi−1>0B_{i-1}>0 and 1<ρ<b1<\rho<b, this share lies strictly between zero and one. This proves the result. □\square

Build external support early so that the full research trajectory can deliver more intrinsic value.
