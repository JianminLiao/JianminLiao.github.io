\textbf{Research in the AI era should pursue intrinsic value while building the external support needed to sustain that pursuit.}

Under the assumptions below, maximizing expected intrinsic value requires an initial phase devoted entirely to external return. An optimal strategy then mixes intrinsic and external work. With enough time, its expected intrinsic allocation share is nondecreasing, even when returns vary randomly across cycles.

Intrinsic value means the scientific understanding or other substantive contribution that the researcher ultimately values. External return means support convertible into research funding, measured in the same units as expenditure. The model treats external support as a means of sustaining research.

\section*{Assumptions}

Consider $n$ research cycles. In cycle $i$, let $c_i$ be expenditure and $p_i\in[0,1]$ the fraction allocated to intrinsic work. Normalize the initial expenditure to $c_1=1$.

\textbf{1. Linear returns with iid random coefficients.} Intrinsic value, external return, and the next research budget satisfy
\[
 I_i=a_i p_i c_i,\qquad E_i=b_i(1-p_i)c_i,\qquad c_{i+1}=d_i E_i.
\]
The vectors $(a_i,b_i,d_i)$ are independent and identically distributed across cycles. Their components may be dependent within a cycle. All coefficients have finite upper bounds, and $a_i$ has a strictly positive lower bound. Write
\[
 b=\operatorname{ess\,inf} b_i,\qquad d_i\ge1\ \text{almost surely},\qquad \mathbb E[d_i]>1.
\]
Thus $b$ is the greatest almost-sure lower bound on the external-return coefficient. The funding conditions mean that external support is fully carried into the next budget and has a positive probability of expanding it. The common distribution stays fixed across cycles, although realized coefficients can change.

\textbf{2. A growing safety balance.} Let the cumulative funding surplus satisfy
\[
 B_0=0,\qquad B_i=B_{i-1}+E_i-c_i.
\]
Require, almost surely in every cycle,
\[
 B_i\ge\rho B_{i-1},\qquad 1<\rho<b.
\]
The multiplier $\rho$ requires any positive reserve to grow. The bound $\rho<b$ makes this requirement feasible and leaves room for intrinsic work. No fixed minimum reserve is imposed. The initial expenditure is supplied as seed funding; balances are assessed at cycle boundaries.

\textbf{3. Decisions from observed resources.} The researcher observes the current budget and balance and chooses $p_i$ before the current coefficients are drawn. Realized coefficients are not directly revealed, even after a cycle. The common coefficient distribution is known, and the sole objective is
\[
 \max\ \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n} I_i\right].
\]
Each cycle's intrinsic value receives equal weight. External return matters through subsequent funding and the safety constraint. The strategy uses observed resources without estimating or reconstructing past coefficients; information about them that may be inferred from resource records is not needed.

\section*{The optimal allocation}

\begin{theorem}
Under these assumptions, there are finite integers $1\le K<N$, depending on the common distribution and the model parameters, with the following property. For every horizon $n\ge N$, every optimal strategy devotes its first $n-K$ cycles entirely to external return:
\[
 p_i=0\qquad(1\le i\le n-K).
\]
There is an optimal strategy that then uses the minimum external share needed to guarantee the safety balance:
\[
 p_i=1-\frac{c_i+(\rho-1)B_{i-1}}{b c_i}\in(0,1)
 \qquad(n-K<i\le n).
\]
For this strategy, the expected intrinsic allocation share is nondecreasing:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0&=\mathbb E[p_1]=\cdots=\mathbb E[p_{n-K}],\\
 0&<\mathbb E[p_{n-K+1}]\le\cdots\le\mathbb E[p_n].
 \end{aligned}
\]
Equivalently, the expected external share $1-\mathbb E[p_i]$ is nonincreasing. The strategy requires only the current budget, balance, cycle number, and the known distribution.
\end{theorem}

The phase structure already holds whenever $n>K$. The first full-external cycle creates a positive reserve, $B_1=b_1-1\ge b-1>0$, which then grows under the safety constraint. The possibly larger threshold $N$ ensures monotonicity of the expected proportions. These expectations average over random histories; individual trajectories can fluctuate. Favorable external returns can leave the realized balance above its required minimum.

\section*{Proof}

\textbf{1. Feasible actions and the Bellman recursion.} Starting from $(c_1,B_0)=(1,0)$, every feasible state satisfies $0\le B\le c$. Indeed, if $B\le c$, then
\[
 B'=B+E-c\le E\le d_iE=c'.
\]
The safety constraint preserves the lower bound. Write $u=(1-p)c$ for the expenditure allocated to external work. At a state $(c,B)$, its feasible interval is exactly
\[
 \frac{c+(\rho-1)B}{b}\le u\le c.
\]
This follows from the essential lower bound $b$ and the almost-sure safety requirement. The interval has positive length because $B\le c$ and $b>\rho$. The same formula applies in the first cycle, when the interval is $[1/b,1]$.

The current coefficient $a_i$ is independent of the information used to choose $p_i$. Dividing the objective by the positive constant $\mathbb E[a_i]$ therefore gives expected stage reward $pc=c-u$. Set
\[
 w=\mathbb E[b_i],\qquad v=\mathbb E[b_i d_i],\qquad v\ge w\ge b>\rho>1.
\]
Induction shows that the optimal normalized expected value with $m$ cycles remaining has the form
\[
 V_m(c,B)=A_m c+D_m B,\qquad A_0=D_0=0.
\]
Define $S_m=vA_m+wD_m$. Given the continuation coefficients, the current objective is
\[
 (1-D_{m-1})c+D_{m-1}B+(S_{m-1}-1)u.
\]
Thus the upper endpoint is uniquely optimal when $S_{m-1}>1$. When $S_{m-1}\le1$, choose the lower endpoint, including at a tie. The lower choice gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A_m&=1-D_{m-1}+\frac{S_{m-1}-1}{b},\\
 D_m&=D_{m-1}+\frac{(\rho-1)(S_{m-1}-1)}{b}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The upper choice gives
\[
 A_m=vA_{m-1}+(w-1)D_{m-1},\qquad D_m=D_{m-1}.
\]
These updates establish the claimed linear value function.

\textbf{2. A single switch between phases.} While the lower endpoint is chosen, $D_m\le0$. Direct substitution gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 S_m={}&\frac{v+w(\rho-1)}{b}S_{m-1}
       +\frac{v(b-1)-w(\rho-1)}{b}\\
       &+(w-v)D_{m-1}
       \ \ge\ \rho S_{m-1}+b-\rho.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Starting from $S_0=0$, this sequence increases until it crosses $1$ in finite time. Let
\[
 K=\min\{m\ge1:S_m>1\}.
\]
After the crossing, the upper update gives
\[
 S_m=vS_{m-1}+(w-v)D_{m-1}>S_{m-1}>1.
\]
The coefficient $D_m$ then remains fixed and nonpositive. Therefore every decision with more than $K$ cycles remaining uniquely chooses $u=c$, and every later decision admits the lower-endpoint optimum. This proves the phase structure and the displayed allocation formula, including the first-cycle decision.

\textbf{3. Monotonicity of the expected proportions.} Write $r=B/c\in[0,1]$. In the mixed phase,
\[
 1-p=\frac{1+(\rho-1)r}{b}.
\]
It therefore suffices to prove that the expected reserve-to-budget ratio decreases during that phase, once the preceding full-external phase is long enough.

For a fresh draw $(b_i,d_i)$, the two state transitions are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F_i(r)&=\frac{r+b_i-1}{b_i d_i}
       &&\text{under full external allocation},\\
 G_i(r)&=\frac{1}{d_i}\left(1-\frac{b}{b_i}
              \frac{1-r}{1+(\rho-1)r}\right)
       &&\text{under mixed allocation}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Both maps preserve $[0,1]$ and are strictly increasing. Each $F_i$ contracts distances by at most $1/b<1$. Iterating the iid maps from an independent infinite past gives a stationary distribution $\pi$. Coupling any other initial state to it using the same draws makes their distance at most $b^{-t}$ after $t$ steps.

If $R$ has distribution $\pi$ and is independent of a fresh coefficient draw, stationarity gives
\[
 \mathbb E[1-R]=
 \frac{\mathbb E[1-1/d_i]}{1-\mathbb E[1/(b_i d_i)]}>0.
\]
The numerator is positive because $d_i\ge1$ and $\mathbb E[d_i]>1$. Moreover,
\[
 F_i(r)-G_i(r)=\frac{1-r}{b_i d_i}
 \left(\frac{b}{1+(\rho-1)r}-1\right)>0
 \qquad(r<1).
\]
Consequently, applying $G_i$ to a stationary starting state produces a distribution that can be coupled below $\pi$, with a strictly smaller mean. Applying the same subsequent draws preserves strict order, because every $G_i$ is strictly increasing. Thus successive mixed-phase distributions started from $\pi$ have strictly decreasing means.

There are only $K$ mixed cycles. As the full-external prefix length $n-K$ grows, its final state distribution converges to $\pi$. The maps $G_i$ have Lipschitz constants at most $\rho$, so each of these finitely many mixed-phase expectations converges to its stationary-start counterpart. All adjacent limiting differences are strictly positive. They therefore remain positive for every sufficiently long prefix. Choose a finite $N>K$ beyond this point. If $K=1$, there are no mixed-phase comparisons and $N=K+1$ suffices.

At the phase switch, the external proportion drops from $1$ to at most $\rho/b<1$. Combining this drop with the mixed-phase comparisons proves the expected monotonicity for every $n\ge N$. Finally, the constructed optimal policy uses only the cycle number and the observed pair $(c_i,B_{i-1})$. Independent draws make this state sufficient for the Bellman recursion. The policy neither observes nor estimates historical coefficient realizations.\hfill$\square$

\medskip
\textbf{Even when intrinsic value is the sole objective, building external support first can be the optimal way to sustain an increasing intrinsic allocation over time in expectation.}
