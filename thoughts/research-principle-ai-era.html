A Research Principle for the AI Era

Jianmin Liao · September 28, 2026

Research in the AI era should pursue intrinsic value while building the external support needed to sustain that pursuit.

Under the assumptions below, maximizing expected intrinsic value requires an initial phase devoted entirely to external return. An optimal strategy then mixes intrinsic and external work. With enough time, its expected intrinsic allocation share is nondecreasing, even when returns vary randomly across cycles.

Intrinsic value means the scientific understanding or other substantive contribution that the researcher ultimately values. External return means support convertible into research funding, measured in the same units as expenditure. The model treats external support as a means of sustaining research.

Assumptions

Consider nn research cycles. In cycle ii, let cic_i be expenditure and pi∈[0,1]p_i\in[0,1] the fraction allocated to intrinsic work. Normalize the initial expenditure to c1=1c_1=1.

1. Linear returns with iid random coefficients. Intrinsic value, external return, and the next research budget satisfy

Ii=aipici,I_i=a_i p_i c_i,
Ei=bi(1−pi)ci,E_i=b_i(1-p_i)c_i,
ci+1=diEi.c_{i+1}=d_i E_i.

The vectors (ai,bi,di)(a_i,b_i,d_i) are independent and identically distributed across cycles. Their components may be dependent within a cycle. All coefficients have finite upper bounds, and aia_i has a strictly positive lower bound. Write

b=ess inf⁡bi,b=\operatorname{ess\,inf} b_i,
di≥1 almost surely,d_i\ge1\ \text{almost surely},
E[di]>1.\mathbb E[d_i]>1.

Thus bb is the greatest almost-sure lower bound on the external-return coefficient. The funding conditions mean that external support is fully carried into the next budget and has a positive probability of expanding it. The common distribution stays fixed across cycles, although realized coefficients can change.

2. A growing safety balance. Let the cumulative funding surplus satisfy

B0=0,B_0=0,
Bi=Bi−1+Ei−ci.B_i=B_{i-1}+E_i-c_i.

Require, almost surely in every cycle,

Bi≥ρBi−1,B_i\ge\rho B_{i-1},
1<ρ<b.1<\rho<b.

The multiplier ρ\rho requires any positive reserve to grow. The bound ρ<b\rho<b makes this requirement feasible and leaves room for intrinsic work. No fixed minimum reserve is imposed. The initial expenditure is supplied as seed funding; balances are assessed at cycle boundaries.

3. Decisions with limited distributional information. The researcher observes the current budget and balance and chooses pip_i before the current coefficients are drawn. Realized coefficients are not directly revealed, even after a cycle. The common distribution need not be known: only bb, E[bi]\mathbb E[b_i], and E[bidi]\mathbb E[b_i d_i] are known. The sole objective is

max⁡ E ⁣[∑i=1nIi].\max\ \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n} I_i\right].

Each cycle's intrinsic value receives equal weight. External return matters through subsequent funding and the safety constraint. The strategy uses observed resources without estimating or reconstructing past coefficients; information about them that may be inferred from resource records is not needed.

The optimal allocation

Theorem

Under these assumptions, there is a finite integer K≥1K\ge1, determined by ρ\rho and the three known quantities. For each admissible common distribution, there is a finite integer N>KN>K such that, for every horizon n≥Nn\ge N, every optimal strategy devotes its first n−Kn-K cycles entirely to external return:

pi=0p_i=0
(1≤i≤n−K).(1\le i\le n-K).

There is an optimal strategy that then uses the minimum external share needed to guarantee the safety balance:

pi=1−ci+(ρ−1)Bi−1bci∈(0,1)p_i=1-\frac{c_i+(\rho-1)B_{i-1}}{b c_i}\in(0,1)
(n−K<i≤n).(n-K<i\le n).

For this strategy, the expected intrinsic allocation share is nondecreasing:

0=E[p1]=⋯=E[pn−K],0<E[pn−K+1]≤⋯≤E[pn].\begin{aligned} 0&=\mathbb E[p_1]=\cdots=\mathbb E[p_{n-K}],\\ 0&<\mathbb E[p_{n-K+1}]\le\cdots\le\mathbb E[p_n]. \end{aligned}

Equivalently, the expected external share 1−E[pi]1-\mathbb E[p_i] is nonincreasing. Given nn and ρ\rho, the strategy uses only the three known quantities, the cycle number, and the current budget and balance.

The phase structure already holds whenever n>Kn>K. The first full-external cycle creates a positive reserve, B1=b1−1≥b−1>0B_1=b_1-1\ge b-1>0, which then grows under the safety constraint. The threshold NN ensures monotonicity of the expected proportions and may depend on the unknown distribution; it is not needed to execute the policy. The theorem does not give a common NN for all admissible distributions. Individual trajectories can fluctuate, and favorable external returns can leave the balance above its required minimum.

3. Monotonicity of the expected proportions. Write r=B/c∈[0,1]r=B/c\in[0,1]. In the mixed phase,

Applying the same subsequent draws preserves strict order, because every GiG_i is strictly increasing. Thus successive mixed-phase distributions started from π\pi have strictly decreasing means.

There are only KK mixed cycles. As the full-external prefix length n−Kn-K grows, its final state distribution converges to π\pi. The maps GiG_i have Lipschitz constants at most ρ\rho, so each of these finitely many mixed-phase expectations converges to its stationary-start counterpart. All adjacent limiting differences are strictly positive. They therefore remain positive for every sufficiently long prefix. Choose a finite N>KN>K beyond this point. If K=1K=1, there are no mixed-phase comparisons and N=K+1N=K+1 suffices.

At the phase switch, the external proportion drops from 11 to at most ρ/b<1\rho/b<1. Combining this drop with the mixed-phase comparisons proves the expected monotonicity for every n≥Nn\ge N. Independent draws make the current budget and balance sufficient for the Bellman recursion. Computing KK and executing the policy require only b,w,v,b,w,v, and ρ\rho, together with the horizon, cycle number, and observed state. Neither the full distribution nor historical coefficient realizations are required. □\square

Even when intrinsic value is the sole objective, building external support first can be the optimal way to sustain an increasing intrinsic allocation over time in expectation.
