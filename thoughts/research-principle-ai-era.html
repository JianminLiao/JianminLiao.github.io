A Research Principle for the AI Era

Jianmin Liao · September 28, 2026

Even when intrinsic value is the sole objective, the optimal strategy starts by investing entirely in external value.

The model below establishes this result for a sufficiently long research horizon.

Intrinsic value is the scientific contribution the researcher ultimately values. External value is support convertible into research funding, measured in the same units as expenditure.

Assumptions

Consider nn research cycles, starting with a seed budget c1=1c_1=1. In cycle ii, expenditure is cic_i and the fraction allocated to intrinsic work is pi∈[0,1]p_i\in[0,1].

1. Linear returns. Intrinsic value, external value, and the next budget satisfy

Ii=aipici,I_i=a_i p_i c_i,
Ei=bi(1−pi)ci,E_i=b_i(1-p_i)c_i,
ci+1=diEi.c_{i+1}=d_iE_i.

The vectors (ai,bi,di)(a_i,b_i,d_i) are iid across cycles. All coefficients are bounded above, and aia_i has a positive lower bound. Write

b=ess inf⁡bi,b=\operatorname{ess\,inf}b_i,
di≥1 almost surely.d_i\ge1\ \text{almost surely}.

External support is fully carried into the next research budget.

2. A growing reserve. The funding balance obeys

B0=0,B_0=0,
Bi=Bi−1+Ei−ci,B_i=B_{i-1}+E_i-c_i,

with the requirement

Bi≥ρBi−1 almost surely,B_i\ge\rho B_{i-1}\ \text{almost surely},
1<ρ<b.1<\rho<b.

Research income must cover spending while growing the reserve.

3. Intrinsic value as the sole objective. The researcher observes the current budget and balance and chooses pip_i before the current coefficients are drawn. Coefficient realizations are not directly revealed. Only bb, E[bi]\mathbb E[b_i], and E[bidi]\mathbb E[b_i d_i] are known about the common distribution. The objective is

max⁡ E ⁣[∑i=1nIi].\max\ \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}I_i\right].

The result

Theorem

There is a finite integer K≥1K\ge1, independent of the horizon nn, such that whenever n>Kn>K, every optimal strategy allocates all resources to external value in the first n−Kn-K cycles:

pi=0p_i=0
(1≤i≤n−K),almost surely.(1\le i\le n-K),\quad\text{almost surely}.

A numerical example

Take n=13n=13, ai=1a_i=1, bi=1.11b_i=1.11, and di=ρ=1.05d_i=\rho=1.05 in every cycle. Here K=10K=10: maximizing total intrinsic value begins with three cycles devoted entirely to external value.

Proof

Every feasible state satisfies 0≤B≤c0\le B\le c: this holds initially and is preserved because

0≤B′=B+E−c≤E≤diE=c′.0\le B'=B+E-c\le E\le d_iE=c'.

Write u=(1−p)cu=(1-p)c for external expenditure. The reserve constraint gives the feasible interval

c+(ρ−1)Bb≤u≤c.\frac{c+(\rho-1)B}{b}\le u\le c.

Independence lets us divide the objective by the positive constant E[ai]\mathbb E[a_i], giving stage reward c−uc-u. Set

w=E[bi],w=\mathbb E[b_i],
v=E[bidi],v=\mathbb E[b_i d_i],
v≥w≥b>ρ>1.v\ge w\ge b>\rho>1.

The value function with mm cycles remaining is linear: Vm(c,B)=Amc+DmBV_m(c,B)=A_m c+D_m B, with A0=D0=0A_0=D_0=0. To see this, define Sm=vAm+wDmS_m=vA_m+wD_m. The Bellman objective is

(1−Dm−1)c+Dm−1B+(Sm−1−1)u.(1-D_{m-1})c+D_{m-1}B+(S_{m-1}-1)u.

Its maximizing endpoint depends only on Sm−1S_{m-1}, and both endpoints are linear in (c,B)(c,B), completing the induction.

When Sm−1≤1S_{m-1}\le1, choose the lower endpoint. Starting from S0=D0=0S_0=D_0=0, this gives

Dm=Dm−1+(ρ−1)(Sm−1−1)b≤0D_m=D_{m-1}+\frac{(\rho-1)(S_{m-1}-1)}{b}\le0

and

Sm=v+w(ρ−1)bSm−1+v(b−1)−w(ρ−1)b+(w−v)Dm−1 ≥ ρSm−1+b−ρ.\begin{aligned} S_m={}&\frac{v+w(\rho-1)}{b}S_{m-1} +\frac{v(b-1)-w(\rho-1)}{b}\\ &+(w-v)D_{m-1} \ \ge\ \rho S_{m-1}+b-\rho. \end{aligned}

Since ρ>1\rho>1 and b>ρb>\rho, this sequence crosses 11 in finite time. Let K=min⁡{m≥1:Sm>1}K=\min\{m\ge1:S_m>1\}.

Once Sm−1>1S_{m-1}>1, the unique maximizing choice is u=cu=c. Then Dm=Dm−1≤0D_m=D_{m-1}\le0 and

Sm=vSm−1+(w−v)Dm−1>Sm−1>1.S_m=vS_{m-1}+(w-v)D_{m-1}>S_{m-1}>1.

Thus every decision with more than KK cycles remaining uniquely allocates everything to external value. These are precisely the first n−Kn-K cycles. □\square

Even when only intrinsic value matters, the optimal starting allocation is entirely external.
